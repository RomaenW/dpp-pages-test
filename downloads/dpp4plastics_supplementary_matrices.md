# DPP4Plastics Matrices for Paper, Poster and Supplement

## Use guidance

- **Paper:** use the Paper Gate Matrix and Paper Friction Matrix.

- **Poster:** use the Poster Summary Matrix and the Poster Gate Flow as source for a visual process graphic.

- **Supplement / QR-code landing page:** use the inverse Detail Matrix, Source Map and Friction Derivation.

- **Method note:** the gate matrix is a controlled consolidation; the friction matrix is an interpretive synthesis and should be labelled accordingly.


## Paper Gate Matrix

| Gate ID | Gate | Lifecycle part | Reference unit | Main actor / role | Main DPP function | Minimum critical data focus | Evidence basis | UNTP / traceability object | Key limitation / friction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G1 | Material Supply | Upstream | Material batch | Polymer or material supplier | Documents material identity, composition, technical specification and material-level compliance before conversion. | Polymer type and grade; batch identifier; additives and fillers; restricted-substance status; relevant material properties; recycled-content status where applicable. | Safety Data Sheet (SDS); Technical Data Sheet (TDS); Regulatory Information Sheet (RIS); supplier declaration; test report; material-level conformity claim. | Material DPP / Digital Material Passport; ConformityClaim; ConformityInformation; ConformityCredential; TransactionEvent where material is transferred. | Formulation and additive data may be commercially sensitive, although downstream actors need hazard- and recyclability-relevant information. |
| G2 | Component Manufacturing | Upstream | Component lot / part batch | Converter / injection moulder / component manufacturer | Transfers material evidence into article- or component-level documentation and records the transformation from input material to component. | Input material batch; component ID; component mass and geometry; processing step; introduced additives or colourants; homogeneous-material relevance; component quality data. | Batch record; process record; manufacturer declaration; XRF or laboratory screening where applicable; component-level conformity claim. | Component DPP; TransformationEvent; ObjectEvent for inspection or testing; TransactionEvent for handover. | Material-level evidence does not automatically prove component-level or article-level compliance after processing. |
| G3 | End Product | Upstream | Product item / product model | OEM / brand owner / actor placing product on the market | Creates the market-facing DPP entry point and aggregates linked material and component documentation into a product-level record. | Bill of materials; linked component/material DPP references; product category; EEE category; product identifiers; disassembly and End-of-Life instructions; market-compliance claims. | Technical file references; declarations; end-product conformity credentials; product-level DPP; linked lower-level credentials. | End-product DPP; AssociationEvent for assembly; TransactionEvent for market handover; Verifiable Credential wrapper. | Market-facing compliance information is not identical to the operational data needed for sorting, treatment and requalification at End-of-Life. |
| G4 | Collection | Downstream | Collection batch | Collector / Producer Responsibility Organisation / take-back or logistics operator | Starts End-of-Life batch documentation by aggregating returned products into managed collection batches. | Collection batch ID; collection date; source/origin; collection point or container ID; total weight; WEEE category; readable product identifier where available. | Product Recycled Batch Log (PRBL) entry; collection event; WEEE/EPR reporting record; chain-of-custody documentation. | PRBL initiated; AggregationEvent; TransactionEvent; end-product DPP remains an information source. | DPP-enabled and non-DPP products will coexist, and physical identifiers may not be read or readable at intake. |
| G5 | Pre-processing | Downstream | Sub-stream / treatment batch | WEEE treatment operator / pre-processor | Converts collected products into technically and legally manageable sub-streams and removes hazardous or disruptive components. | Input collection batch; removed batteries, printed circuit boards and critical components; dismantling steps; separation rates; hazardous-part share; initial screenings. | Treatment log; pre-processing event; screening record; facility record; object-level inspection or testing evidence. | PRBL updated; TransformationEvent; ObjectEvent; component/product DPP can still support treatment decisions. | Product identity begins to break down as products are opened, dismantled, mixed or size-reduced. |
| G6 | Sorting | Downstream | Polymer fraction | Sorter / recycling pre-treatment operator | Classifies mixed plastic sub-streams into material fractions suitable for rejection, downcycling or further recycling. | Fraction ID; polymer type; colour; NIR/XRF result; contamination level; purity; hazardous-substance flags; yield; input/output mass balance. | Sorting log; screening report; laboratory report; object event; fraction-level quality evidence. | PRBL updated; TransformationEvent; ObjectEvent; physical product-level DPP is no longer reliable after mixing/shredding. | Item-level DPP logic becomes insufficient for fraction-level decisions once physical identity is lost. |
| G7 | Compounding | Downstream / re-entry | Recyclate output batch | Recycler / compounder / recycled material producer | Qualifies the recycled material and creates a new material-level DPP for the recyclate batch. | Regranulate batch ID; input fraction IDs and shares; blending model; additives; process data; test results; Data Quality Level; recycled content; conformity status. | Certificate of Analysis (CoA); DQL datasheet according to EN 18065 logic; SDS/RIS; laboratory reports; recyclate conformity claims. | PRBL finalized; TransformationEvent; ObjectEvent; TransactionEvent; new material DPP for the recyclate batch. | Recyclate claims require new batch-level testing and evidence; they cannot simply inherit upstream product claims. |



## Paper Friction Matrix

| ID | Friction mechanism | Affected gates | Core tension | Consequence for DPP use | Required clarification / governance implication | Source basis | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F1 | Product identity vs. batch/fraction reality | G3-G7 | Products are documented as identifiable items or models, while End-of-Life operations increasingly operate with collection batches, sub-streams, fractions and recyclate output batches. | A continuous product-level identity cannot carry the whole recycling process after physical transformation. | Define rules for linking product DPPs to PRBL entries, downstream events, fraction IDs and newly generated recyclate DPPs. | Tables 16 and 19; downstream gate texts; Annex 7/8 examples. | Interpretive synthesis |
| F2 | Compliance evidence vs. operational recycling data | G1-G7 | Upstream documentation is mainly compliance- and specification-oriented, whereas recyclers require decision-ready data for dismantling, sorting, screening and material qualification. | Data may exist but still not support operational routing or material requalification. | Specify minimum downstream-relevant data fields and connect them to evidence objects, not only to generic product information. | Tables 13-19; sections on DPP content, workshops and End-of-Life gates. | Interpretive synthesis |
| F3 | Confidentiality vs. downstream need-to-know | G1-G3; G5-G7 | Formulations, additives and supplier data are commercially sensitive, but hazard flags and recyclability information are relevant for downstream actors. | Either too little disclosure reduces DPP usefulness, or excessive disclosure creates intellectual-property and liability concerns. | Use role-based access, protected attributes, and disclosure levels that expose hazard/recycling relevance without fully exposing formulations. | Regulatory/technology sections on access rights, interoperability and supplier enablement. | Interpretive synthesis |
| F4 | Physical readability vs. digital availability | G3-G6 | DPP information only helps if the product, component or batch can still be physically identified or linked to a digital record. | Products may enter anonymous mixed streams, and data carriers can be damaged, unread or not operationally scanned. | Define robust carrier strategies and fallback identification logic for collection and pre-treatment. | Tables 6-7; technology sections on data carriers and physical marking; downstream gate texts. | Interpretive synthesis |
| F5 | Inherited claims vs. requalified material | G6-G7 | Recycled material is transformed, mixed and reprocessed, so upstream product or component claims cannot automatically establish recyclate conformity. | The recyclate needs new batch-level evidence before it can re-enter material supply. | Define evidence requirements for recyclate conformity, DQL documentation, CoA and new material DPP creation. | Tables 17-18; Table 16; Annex 8 recyclate DPP example. | Interpretive synthesis |
| F6 | Standard data model vs. actor-specific IT and practice | All gates | DPP standards define data structures, while actors work with ERP, PLM, QA records, lab reports, EPR systems and recycler treatment logs. | Implementation remains fragmented, manual or platform-specific if semantic and technical interoperability is not operationalised. | Develop sector-specific profiles, templates and mapping rules between existing operational systems and DPP data objects. | Tables 5-6; survey/workshop findings; recommendations on data infrastructure. | Interpretive synthesis |
| F7 | Product-law status vs. waste/material status | G3-G7 | The same physical object moves from product law into waste-management logic and finally into secondary material qualification. | DPP governance cannot rely on one legal reference frame only. | Clarify how product, waste, chemical and secondary-material obligations connect across lifecycle transitions. | Regulatory sections on EEE, WEEE, REACH/RoHS/POPs, policy coordination; downstream sections. | Interpretive synthesis |
| F8 | Assurance burden vs. feasibility for smaller actors | G1-G7; horizontal actors | Reliable DPP claims require evidence, testing, credentials and auditability, but implementation cost and competence requirements may exceed the capacity of smaller actors. | High assurance demands may slow adoption; weak assurance increases greenwashing and non-compliance risks. | Provide templates, shared sector profiles, proportional evidence requirements and clear responsibilities for claim issuance and verification. | Survey results, double materiality tables 20-26, recommendations on SME support and auditing. | Interpretive synthesis |



## Poster Summary Matrix

| Data logic | Upstream: G1-G3 | Transition: G4 | Downstream: G5-G7 | Poster message |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reference unit | Material batch -> component lot -> product item/model | Collection batch | Sub-stream -> fraction -> recyclate output batch | The DPP reference object changes across the lifecycle. |
| DPP role | Documentation, compliance evidence and product passport creation | Bridge from product identity to End-of-Life batch documentation | Treatment support, fraction classification and recyclate requalification | The DPP is not one static product record; it becomes a chain of linked evidence objects. |
| Core data | Composition, specification, bill of materials, conformity and design-for-treatment information | Product category, collection batch, source, weight and readable identifiers | Treatment steps, screening, sorting results, quality tests, yield, DQL and CoA | Data must be selected by operational decision, not by generic availability. |
| Evidence | SDS, TDS, RIS, supplier declarations, conformity credentials, technical file references | PRBL, collection event, WEEE/EPR reporting, chain-of-custody records | PRBL updates, screening reports, sorting logs, lab reports, CoA, recyclate DPP | Claims need evidence and a clear reference unit. |
| Main friction | Confidentiality and transferability of evidence | DPP and non-DPP product streams coexist | Loss of item identity and need for new batch-level evidence | The central discontinuity appears at End-of-Life. |



## Poster Gate Flow

| Zone | Gate | Reference unit | Main evidence object | Main message |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Upstream | G1 Material Supply | Material batch | Material DPP / SDS / TDS / conformity claims | Material identity and compliance are established before conversion. |
| Upstream | G2 Component Manufacturing | Component lot / part batch | Component DPP / process record / TransformationEvent | Material evidence is transformed into component-level evidence. |
| Upstream | G3 End Product | Product item / model | End-product DPP / AssociationEvent / technical file | The product passport becomes the market-facing entry point. |
| Transition | G4 Collection | Collection batch | PRBL initiated / AggregationEvent / TransactionEvent | Product identity is linked to End-of-Life batch logic where possible. |
| Downstream | G5 Pre-processing | Sub-stream / treatment batch | PRBL updated / TransformationEvent / treatment log | Products are opened and converted into manageable sub-streams. |
| Downstream | G6 Sorting | Polymer fraction | PRBL updated / sorting log / screening report | Physical item identity is no longer the operative reference. |
| Downstream | G7 Compounding | Recyclate output batch | PRBL finalized / CoA / DQL / new material DPP | A new material DPP is generated for the recyclate batch. |

## Supplement S3 - Friction Derivation

| Friction ID | Friction mechanism | Source evidence / report basis | Interpretation step | Implication for paper |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F1 | Product identity vs. batch/fraction reality | Table 16 explicitly shifts downstream operational units from collection batch to sub-streams, fractions and output batch; Table 19 summarizes supply-chain perspective. | Translate the operational-unit shift into a lifecycle discontinuity between product DPP identity and recycling process reality. | Use as central argument for why gate- and reference-unit-based DPP design is needed. |
| F2 | Compliance evidence vs. operational recycling data | Tables 13-15 focus on material/component/product conformity claims; downstream sections add sorting, screening, treatment and qualification needs. | Compare upstream claim logic with downstream operational decision needs. | Use to justify minimum critical data fields beyond generic compliance records. |
| F3 | Confidentiality vs. downstream need-to-know | Report discusses data access, role-based rights, material composition and need for recyclers to receive relevant information. | Derive tension between supplier protection and downstream hazard/recyclability information. | Use in discussion/governance implications, not as a purely extracted finding. |
| F4 | Physical readability vs. digital availability | Technology chapter discusses data carriers/marking; downstream sections require physical identification and batch linking. | A digital record is only useful at EoL if it can be linked to the physical item or batch. | Use for poster callout and discussion of implementation limits. |
| F5 | Inherited claims vs. requalified material | Tables 17-18 and Annex 8 show recyclate-level claims, DQL data and new DPP for ABS regranulate. | Recyclate evidence is not inherited from products; it is generated through testing and qualification of the output batch. | Use as key downstream result. |
| F6 | Standard data model vs. actor-specific IT and practice | Standardization and technology chapters plus workshop/survey findings show interoperability and operational-readiness issues. | Move from formal DPP model to practical implementation problem. | Use to link results to broader DPP implementation literature. |
| F7 | Product-law status vs. waste/material status | Regulatory chapters discuss product, chemical and waste-law frameworks; downstream sections move from product to waste and recyclate. | The DPP must bridge multiple regulatory reference frames across lifecycle status changes. | Use to define 'normative friction' rather than only technical friction. |
| F8 | Assurance burden vs. feasibility for smaller actors | Survey, double-materiality and recommendations discuss organisational preparedness, auditing, capability and cost concerns. | Reliable evidence chains increase implementation burden. | Use carefully; frame as adoption/governance friction. |
