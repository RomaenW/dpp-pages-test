Reference-unit shift across the lifecycle

The practical DPP logic changes because the relevant unit changes from material batch to component lot, product item, collection batch, sub-stream, fraction and recyclate output batch.

G1Material SupplyMaterial batch
G2Component ManufacturingComponent lot / part batch
G3End ProductProduct item / product model
G4CollectionCollection batch
G5Pre-processingSub-stream / treatment batch
G6SortingPolymer fraction
G7CompoundingRecyclate output batch

Gate details

These cards expand the compact poster graphic. Open a gate to see data focus, evidence basis, traceability object and the main limitation.

G1 Material SupplyMaterial batch · Upstream
Reference unit
Material batch
Main actor
Polymer or material supplier
Main DPP role
Documents material identity, composition, technical specification and material-level compliance before conversion.
Minimum data focus
Polymer type and grade; batch identifier; additives and fillers; restricted-substance status; relevant material properties; recycled-content status where applicable.
Evidence basis
Safety Data Sheet (SDS); Technical Data Sheet (TDS); Regulatory Information Sheet (RIS); supplier declaration; test report; material-level conformity claim.
UNTP / traceability object
Material Digital Product Passport (DPP) or Digital Material Passport; ConformityClaim; ConformityInformation; ConformityCredential; TransactionEvent where material is transferred.
Operational decision supported
Can this material be used, documented and transferred into component manufacturing?
Main limitation
Formulation and additive data may be commercially sensitive, although downstream actors need hazard- and recyclability-relevant information.
G2 Component ManufacturingComponent lot / part batch · Upstream
Reference unit
Component lot / part batch
Main actor
Converter, injection moulder or component manufacturer
Main DPP role
Transfers material evidence into article- or component-level documentation and records the transformation from input material to component.
Minimum data focus
Input material batch; component ID; component mass and geometry; processing step; introduced additives or colourants; homogeneous-material relevance; component quality data.
Evidence basis
Batch record; process record; manufacturer declaration; X-ray fluorescence (XRF) or laboratory screening where applicable; component-level conformity claim.
UNTP / traceability object
Component DPP; TransformationEvent; ObjectEvent for inspection or testing; TransactionEvent for handover.
Operational decision supported
Can this component inherit sufficient material evidence and add component-specific proof?
Main limitation
Material-level evidence does not automatically prove component-level or article-level compliance after processing.
G3 End ProductProduct item / product model · Upstream
Reference unit
Product item / product model
Main actor
Original Equipment Manufacturer (OEM), brand owner or actor placing the product on the market
Main DPP role
Creates the market-facing DPP entry point and aggregates linked material and component documentation into a product-level record.
Minimum data focus
Bill of materials; linked component and material DPP references; product category; Electrical and Electronic Equipment (EEE) category; product identifiers; disassembly and End-of-Life instructions; market-compliance claims.
Evidence basis
Technical file references; declarations; end-product conformity credentials; product-level DPP; linked lower-level credentials.
UNTP / traceability object
End-product DPP; AssociationEvent for assembly; TransactionEvent for market handover; Verifiable Credential wrapper.
Operational decision supported
Can the product be placed on the market and later identified or linked to End-of-Life treatment?
Main limitation
Market-facing compliance information is not identical to the operational data needed for sorting, treatment and requalification at End-of-Life.
G4 CollectionCollection batch · Downstream
Reference unit
Collection batch
Main actor
Collector, Producer Responsibility Organisation, take-back or logistics operator
Main DPP role
Starts End-of-Life batch documentation by aggregating returned products into managed collection batches.
Minimum data focus
Collection batch ID; collection date; source or origin; collection point or container ID; total weight; WEEE category; readable product identifier where available.
Evidence basis
Product Recycled Batch Log (PRBL) entry; collection event; Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment (WEEE) / Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) reporting record; chain-of-custody documentation.
UNTP / traceability object
PRBL initiated; AggregationEvent; TransactionEvent; end-product DPP remains an information source.
Operational decision supported
Can returned products be grouped, recorded and routed into the downstream treatment chain?
Main limitation
DPP-enabled and non-DPP products will coexist, and physical identifiers may not be read or readable at intake.
G5 Pre-processingSub-stream / treatment batch · Downstream
Reference unit
Sub-stream / treatment batch
Main actor
WEEE treatment operator or pre-processor
Main DPP role
Converts collected products into technically and legally manageable sub-streams and removes hazardous or disruptive components.
Minimum data focus
Input collection batch; removed batteries, printed circuit boards and critical components; dismantling steps; separation rates; hazardous-part share; initial screenings.
Evidence basis
Treatment log; pre-processing event; screening record; facility record; object-level inspection or testing evidence.
UNTP / traceability object
PRBL updated; TransformationEvent; ObjectEvent; component or product DPP can still support treatment decisions.
Operational decision supported
Which parts must be removed before sorting and which sub-streams can be created?
Main limitation
Product identity begins to break down as products are opened, dismantled, mixed or size-reduced.
G6 SortingPolymer fraction · Downstream
Reference unit
Polymer fraction
Main actor
Sorter or recycling pre-treatment operator
Main DPP role
Classifies mixed plastic sub-streams into material fractions suitable for rejection, downcycling or further recycling.
Minimum data focus
Fraction ID; polymer type; colour; near-infrared (NIR) / XRF result; contamination level; purity; hazardous-substance flags; yield; input/output mass balance.
Evidence basis
Sorting log; screening report; laboratory report; object event; fraction-level quality evidence.
UNTP / traceability object
PRBL updated; TransformationEvent; ObjectEvent; physical product-level DPP is no longer reliable after mixing or shredding.
Operational decision supported
Which fractions can be recycled, rejected, downcycled or forwarded to compounding?
Main limitation
Item-level DPP logic becomes insufficient for fraction-level decisions once physical identity is lost.
G7 CompoundingRecyclate output batch · Downstream / re-entry
Reference unit
Recyclate output batch
Main actor
Recycler, compounder or recycled material producer
Main DPP role
Qualifies the recycled material and creates a new material-level DPP for the recyclate batch.
Minimum data focus
Regranulate batch ID; input fraction IDs and shares; blending model; additives; process data; test results; Data Quality Level (DQL); recycled content; conformity status.
Evidence basis
Certificate of Analysis (CoA); DQL datasheet according to EN 18065 logic; SDS/RIS; laboratory reports; recyclate conformity claims.
UNTP / traceability object
PRBL finalized; TransformationEvent; ObjectEvent; TransactionEvent; new material DPP for the recyclate batch.
Operational decision supported
Can the recyclate be sold as qualified secondary material and re-enter material supply?
Main limitation
Recyclate claims require new batch-level testing and evidence; they cannot simply inherit upstream product claims.

Lifecycle frictions

These are not direct table copies. They are condensed problem mechanisms derived from the gate logic, downstream PRBL logic, technology constraints and regulatory context.

F1 Product identity vs. batch/fraction realityG3-G7
Affected gates
G3-G7
Core tension
Products are documented as identifiable items or models, while End-of-Life operations increasingly operate with collection batches, sub-streams, fractions and recyclate output batches.
Consequence for DPP use
A continuous product-level identity cannot carry the whole recycling process after physical transformation.
Governance clarification needed
Define rules for linking product DPPs to PRBL entries, downstream events, fraction IDs and newly generated recyclate DPPs.
F2 Compliance evidence vs. operational recycling dataG1-G7
Affected gates
G1-G7
Core tension
Upstream documentation is mainly compliance- and specification-oriented, whereas recyclers require decision-ready data for dismantling, sorting, screening and material qualification.
Consequence for DPP use
Data may exist but still not support operational routing or material requalification.
Governance clarification needed
Specify minimum downstream-relevant data fields and connect them to evidence objects, not only to generic product information.
F3 Confidentiality vs. downstream need-to-knowG1-G3; G5-G7
Affected gates
G1-G3; G5-G7
Core tension
Formulations, additives and supplier data are commercially sensitive, but hazard flags and recyclability information are relevant for downstream actors.
Consequence for DPP use
Either too little disclosure reduces DPP usefulness, or excessive disclosure creates intellectual-property and liability concerns.
Governance clarification needed
Use role-based access, protected attributes and disclosure levels that expose hazard/recycling relevance without fully exposing formulations.
F4 Physical readability vs. digital availabilityG3-G6
Affected gates
G3-G6
Core tension
DPP information only helps if the product, component or batch can still be physically identified or linked to a digital record.
Consequence for DPP use
Products may enter anonymous mixed streams, and data carriers can be damaged, unread or not operationally scanned.
Governance clarification needed
Define robust carrier strategies and fallback identification logic for collection and pre-treatment.
F5 Inherited claims vs. requalified materialG6-G7
Affected gates
G6-G7
Core tension
Recycled material is transformed, mixed and reprocessed, so upstream product or component claims cannot automatically establish recyclate conformity.
Consequence for DPP use
The recyclate needs new batch-level evidence before it can re-enter material supply.
Governance clarification needed
Define evidence requirements for recyclate conformity, DQL documentation, CoA and new material DPP creation.
F6 Standard data model vs. actor-specific IT and practiceAll gates
Affected gates
All gates
Core tension
DPP standards define data structures, while actors work with Enterprise Resource Planning, Product Lifecycle Management, quality assurance records, lab reports, EPR systems and recycler treatment logs.
Consequence for DPP use
Implementation remains fragmented, manual or platform-specific if semantic and technical interoperability is not operationalised.
Governance clarification needed
Develop sector-specific profiles, templates and mapping rules between existing operational systems and DPP data objects.
F7 Product-law status vs. waste/material statusG3-G7
Affected gates
G3-G7
Core tension
The same physical object moves from product law into waste-management logic and finally into secondary material qualification.
Consequence for DPP use
DPP governance cannot rely on one legal reference frame across the whole lifecycle.
Governance clarification needed
Clarify how product, waste, chemical and secondary-material obligations are linked through events, claims and evidence.
F8 Assurance burden vs. feasibility for smaller actorsAll gates, especially G2-G7
Affected gates
All gates, especially G2-G7
Core tension
Reliable DPPs require evidence, testing, audits and data upkeep, but smaller actors may lack staff, budget or IT integration capacity.
Consequence for DPP use
A technically sound DPP model can still fail in adoption if assurance duties are too burdensome or poorly phased.
Governance clarification needed
Use phased minimum datasets, implementation templates and proportionate evidence requirements.

How the matrices were derived

Source basis

Primary basis: report Tables 13–19 and the accompanying gate descriptions in Section 5.4. Actor-impact and implementation limits are informed by Tables 20–26 and the discussion sections.

Consolidation

The gate cards are controlled consolidations. They group extracted table content and nearby explanatory text by lifecycle gate, reference unit, evidence basis and DPP object.

Interpretation

The friction cards are analytical syntheses. They should be described in the paper as derived categories, not as verbatim entries from the report.
